# Quavok audio assets

Audio is optional and begins after a visitor chooses Start with sound or explicitly requests a reply. The website uses no microphone, speech API, voice backend or browser-installed speech voices. All lines are authored examples. Only the requested clip is fetched. The header sound control mutes both voice and effects.

## Voice

`src/lib/voice-catalog.json` is the catalogue for copy, clip paths, duration and section association. The hero and companion read these same lines (the companion's quick answers come from the catalogue by id), so spoken and written copy cannot drift. Twenty short mono MP3s in `public/media/voice/` total about 780 KB: 24 kHz, 64 kbps, normalized to −18 LUFS with a −1.5 dB true-peak ceiling. A silent 100ms WAV primes the shared media element on an explicit sound gesture when needed.

Generated offline with [Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) (Apache-2.0) through [kokoro-onnx](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) using the v1.0 model files. Rocket uses `af_heart` at 0.9× speed for a calm, unhurried delivery; the visitor uses `am_michael` at 0.95×, so the two speakers are distinct voices rather than pitch-shifted copies. Only generated audio is shipped; no model, runtime or service dependency is part of the website. Attribution accompanies the clips in `CREDITS.txt`. This is a synthetic voice, not a founder or customer recording.

For regeneration, synthesize each catalogue text (curly apostrophes replaced with straight ones), then encode with FFmpeg: `silenceremove` on the lead-in, `highpass=f=70`, a light `deesser`, `lowpass=f=10500`, `loudnorm=I=-18:TP=-1.5:LRA=8`, a 120 ms tail pad, MP3 64k at 24 kHz. Measure the final clip duration with ffprobe and update the catalogue. Write lines the way a person would say them: contractions, short sentences, commas where a breath belongs. Do not add claims, counts or timing promises.

One shared media player owns the sequence. The opening command finishes before the build begins. Once Rocket has travelled into the hero window, the person asks, then Rocket answers. Question selection cancels the old sequence. Scrolling away cancels the hero voice. Section guides wait 850ms for a stable section, speak once per section, and cancel on a new section. Chat has higher priority. Hiding the tab or muting stops playback. Mouth animation follows the playing/ended events, with a visual-only fallback for silent playback.

## Mechanical keys and UI cues

Six mono WAVs in `public/media/keyboard/` (`key-1.wav` … `key-6.wav`, about 71 KB total) are real keystrokes. They were cut from [“Mechanical Keyboard Typing” by shaunhillyard](https://freesound.org/people/shaunhillyard/sounds/830496/) on Freesound, a close-miked recording released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). CC0 needs no attribution, but the source is credited in `public/media/voice/CREDITS.txt`. The switch type is not stated on the source page. The recording is a muted, low-mid keyboard: under 10% of each clip's energy is above 3 kHz.

The working source was the public HQ preview MP3 (`cdn.freesound.org/previews/830/830496_4759831-hq.mp3`). The two channels were identical, so FFmpeg downmixed them to mono, resampled to 44.1 kHz and applied a 70 Hz 2-pole high-pass. Keystroke onsets were found from a 1 ms RMS envelope. Only isolated presses were kept: each has at least 30 ms of quiet before it and no second transient within 22 dB of its peak inside the clip. Each clip starts 2 ms before the transient and runs 120–150 ms. It has a 3 ms raised-cosine fade-in and a 20 ms fade-out, and is peak-normalized to −6 dBFS (RMS −26.5 to −30.4 dBFS). Clips are 16-bit PCM with TPDF dither. In this recording the upstroke was not separable from neighbouring keys, so the clips are presses only. `key-6` is the heaviest, low-frequency stroke (spacebar-like), with about 56% of its energy below 300 Hz. Spaces in the typed text play `key-6`; other keystrokes pick from `key-1`…`key-5` without repeating the previous sample. Each press varies rate by ±4%, gain by about 20%, and lands up to 14 ms off the animation grid, so the typing reads as a person rather than a metronome.

These samples play only while `html.qa-building` is present. Rocket’s answers and section narration never trigger typing sounds. One AudioContext decodes the six small files on sound activation, with a master gain of 0.32. Quiet placement, selection and completion cues use short local oscillators. There is no background music.

## Verification

The browser QA drives the real AudioContext and HTMLAudioElement. It verifies decoded waveform energy, advancing voice playback, completed clips, command/build/question/answer ordering, one context, cancellation, mute, and zero keystrokes outside the build. It also checks the eight routes at phone, tablet and desktop sizes, keyboard use, reduced motion, console errors and axe. QA reports and captures are ignored under `.qa/voiced/`.
