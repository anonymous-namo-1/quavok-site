# Quavok audio assets

Audio is optional and begins after a visitor chooses Start with sound or explicitly requests a reply. The website uses no microphone, speech API, voice backend or browser-installed speech voices. All lines are authored examples. Only the requested clip is fetched. The header sound control mutes both voice and effects.

## Voice

`src/lib/voice-catalog.json` is the catalogue for spoken copy, clip paths and duration. The hero and companion read these same lines (the companion's quick answers come from the catalogue by id), so spoken and written copy cannot drift. Eleven short mono MP3s in `public/media/voice/` (about 425 KB): 24 kHz, 64 kbps, a 120 ms lead-in so the first syllable is never clipped, normalized to −18 LUFS with a −1.5 dB true-peak ceiling. A silent 100ms WAV primes the shared media element on an explicit sound gesture when needed.

Generated offline with [Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) (Apache-2.0) through [kokoro-onnx](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) using the v1.0 model files. Rocket is male and uses `am_michael` at 0.92× speed for a calm, unhurried delivery; the visitor uses the deeper `am_fenrir` at 1.0×, so the two speakers are distinct male voices rather than pitch-shifted copies. Only generated audio is shipped; no model, runtime or service dependency is part of the website. Attribution accompanies the clips in `CREDITS.txt`. This is a synthetic voice, not a founder or customer recording.

For regeneration, synthesize each catalogue text (curly apostrophes replaced with straight ones), then encode with FFmpeg: `silenceremove` on the lead-in, `adelay=120:all=1`, `highpass=f=70`, a light `deesser`, `lowpass=f=10500`, `loudnorm=I=-18:TP=-1.5:LRA=8`, a 120 ms tail pad, MP3 64k at 24 kHz. Measure the final clip duration with ffprobe and update the catalogue. Write lines the way a person would say them: contractions, short sentences, commas where a breath belongs. Do not add claims, counts or timing promises.

Rocket only speaks when someone asks him something. The opening command plays once after Start with sound. After the build, the hero example is spoken if the visitor chose Start with sound, and runs silently otherwise (words appear on screen). Picking question 1, 2 or 3, tapping Rocket in the hero, or Hear reply plays the visitor's question and Rocket's answer. In the companion, choosing a quick question speaks the answer; opening the chat is silent. Section explanations are text bubbles only, shown once per section, and never spoken. Asked-for replies play even if site sound was never switched on, unless the visitor has explicitly chosen silence (header mute or Continue silently). One shared media player owns playback; a new question cancels the old one, and hiding the tab or muting stops it.

## Mechanical keys and UI cues

Six mono WAVs in `public/media/keyboard/` (`key-1.wav` … `key-6.wav`, about 86 KB total) are real keystrokes. The target sound is a NovelKeys Cream–style board: deep, smooth and low-pitched. No Freesound or Wikimedia Commons recording labelled as Cream or POM switches was found, so the clips come from the closest-sounding CC0 recording: [“Typing on a rainy 75” by Techrul](https://freesound.org/people/Techrul/sounds/815614/), released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and tagged creamy/thocky by its uploader. It is a Rainy 75 keyboard, and the page does not name the switches. CC0 needs no attribution, but the source is credited in `public/media/voice/CREDITS.txt`.

The working source was the public HQ preview MP3 (`cdn.freesound.org/previews/815/815614_15899953-hq.mp3`, 48 kHz). Its channels were effectively identical (correlation 0.99999). FFmpeg downmixed it to mono, resampled it to 44.1 kHz and applied this chain: `highpass=f=70:poles=2`, then `equalizer=f=300:t=q:w=1:g=1.5` (+1.5 dB low-mid), then `highshelf=f=4000:t=q:w=0.7:g=-2.5` (−2.5 dB above about 4 kHz). Keystroke onsets were found on the high-passed signal using a 1 ms RMS envelope. For each candidate, the 25 ms before the hit was at least 13 dB below it, and no other key fell inside the clip. Of these, the deepest isolated presses were chosen, so the clips are darker than the recording's typical keystroke. Each clip starts 2 ms before the transient and runs 128–180 ms, long enough to keep the case's low ring. It has a 3 ms raised-cosine fade-in and a 20 ms fade-out, and is peak-normalized to −6 dBFS (RMS −23.9 to −27.7 dBFS). Clips are 16-bit PCM with TPDF dither. Each press has a second, quieter bump 26–47 ms after the hit, at 11–21 dB below the peak, which is part of the same stroke. `key-5` also keeps a soft upstroke at about 108 ms, about 26 dB below the hit. `key-6` is the loudest of the deep strokes in the source (spacebar-like) and is used as the heavy key. Across the six clips about 84% of the energy is below 1 kHz and about 1% is above 3 kHz; the spectral centroid is about 515 Hz. The previous set measured about 19% below 1 kHz, 31% above 3 kHz and a 2.8 kHz centroid. Spaces in the typed text play `key-6`; other keystrokes pick from `key-1`…`key-5` without repeating the previous sample. Each press varies rate by ±4%, gain by about 20%, and lands up to 14 ms off the animation grid, so the typing reads as a person rather than a metronome.

These samples play only while `html.qa-building` is present. Rocket’s answers and section narration never trigger typing sounds. One AudioContext decodes the six small files on sound activation, with a master gain of 0.32. Quiet placement, selection and completion cues use short local oscillators. When the confetti bursts at the end of the build, a soft celebration plays: four quiet sine bells rising through G major (G5, B5, D6, G6, 75 ms apart, each with a faint octave partial and a 0.9 s decay) over a 0.55 s band-passed noise shimmer around 6.5 kHz. The plain completion chime is skipped if the celebration played in the last four seconds. There is no background music.

## Verification

The browser QA drives the real AudioContext and HTMLAudioElement. It verifies decoded waveform energy, advancing voice playback, completed clips, command/build/question/answer ordering, one context, cancellation, mute, and zero keystrokes outside the build. It also checks the eight routes at phone, tablet and desktop sizes, keyboard use, reduced motion, console errors and axe. QA reports and captures are ignored under `.qa/voiced/`.
